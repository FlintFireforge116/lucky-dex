TARGET DESIGN

The game remains PORTRAIT on the phone.

When I previously said “landscape”, I meant the ENVIRONMENT should be viewed horizontally/downrange, NOT that the gameplay should become a wide side-on landscape panel.

The desired composition is:

- Pokémon positioned low near the bottom of the playfield.
- Pokémon fires UP the phone toward targets further into the scene.
- The environment should look like a real range extending away from the player.
- Targets appear progressively further up the screen according to distance.
- Perspective/depth should be immediately obvious.
- The game should feel calm and deliberate, not like a race.

Think Wii Sports Resort Archery composition/feel adapted into the existing Pokémon/Goldenrod visual style.

DO NOT literally copy Nintendo artwork/assets/UI. Reproduce the gameplay feel, camera logic, pacing and readability using LuckyDex assets/styles.

CURRENT PROBLEMS TO FIX

1. The v0.42.2 perspective is still wrong.
   It looks like a flat horizontal banner rather than a player looking down a range.

2. Pokémon is too high in the scene.
   Move it significantly lower and have attacks clearly launch upward from it.

3. The background composition makes little visual sense.
   Create a coherent themed shooting range with foreground, middle distance and background.

5. The reticle is still too prominent.
   Make it smaller, cleaner and precise.

   This is NOT a race.

   Slightly reduce projectile speed.
   It should visibly launch from the Pokémon, travel through the scene, and arrive at the SAME impact coordinate used for scoring.
   The flight should feel smooth and natural rather than like a sprite tween.

NEW HOLD / RELEASE MECHANIC

Change the current focus mechanic.

While the player holds:

- The focus/charge meter should rise toward its ideal point.
- If the player keeps holding, it should then fall back down.
- Continue oscillating while held.
- The player must time RELEASE.

This is an intentional timing mechanic.

A well-timed release near the ideal point should:
- maximise accuracy/stability
- give the most reliable trajectory
- give the strongest clean hit

A poorly timed release should add an appropriate accuracy/stability penalty.

Do NOT make this brutally difficult on low divisions/exhibition Easy.

Division/difficulty should affect things like:
- oscillation speed
- size of the ideal release window
- wind
- distance
- moving-target complexity

But even Master should remain readable and fair.
